How a shared secret works

Shamir's Secret Sharing splits the encryption key into \(n\) shares and hands one to each trusted party. The file can only be decrypted when at least \(k\) of those shares are brought back together — any fewer reveal nothing at all, not even with unlimited computing power.

This enforces separation of duties: no single person can unlock the file alone, a quorum of \(k\) keyholders must cooperate. For example \(k = 2,\ n = 3\) means any 2 of 3 people can decrypt, and you can still recover if one share is lost.
